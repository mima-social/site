1The real first post, its author's site link and their photo are listed in the pull request as things still to supply.

The sleeve

The dark block at the top is the sleeve. It holds the category, the title, a one-sentence deck and the hero figure. The lime card across its foot is the credits band: who wrote the post, their role, what shipped, the pull requests or issues behind it, the reading time and the date.

The figure can carry three to five numbered callouts, and the text refers to them by number. In the stand-in figure above, callout 1 marks the network filters, callout 2 the switch between Timeline and Board, and callout 3 the composer.2

2Callouts are set in front matter, as a point on the figure, a place for the numbered disc and a short label. See the issue for the design.

What a post is made of

A post is one Markdown file in labs-src/posts/. Its front matter fills the sleeve and the credits band. This is the front matter of the page you are reading, shortened:

---
title: How a Labs post is put together
slug: how-a-labs-post-is-built
category: Engineering
date: 2026-11-23
author: dave-monk
draft: true
refs: site#44
figure: /site-assets/app/board.webp
callout-1: 46 10 from 54 0 | Network filters
---

68chthe width of the reading column, the same measure Learn uses

An author is defined once in labs-src/authors.json and reused by every post they write.3 The build is the same script that builds Learn, and it uses only the Python standard library.

3An author with no photo gets a disc with their initials, and an author with no link gets no link.

FieldNeededWhat it does
title, deckYesThe sleeve's headline and the sentence under it
categoryYesThe lime line above the title
authorYesA key in authors.json
shipped, refsNoTwo cells of the credits band
compactNoThe smaller sleeve for a short post
draftNoKeeps the post out of the index, feed, sitemap and llms.txt

Notes in the margin

A note is written as a Markdown footnote. On a wide screen it sits in the margin beside the paragraph that cites it. On a narrow screen it follows that paragraph.

Note: a number in a post is either real or labelled as illustrative. The big number in the margin is real: it is the column width set in the stylesheet.

Lists, tables, code and callouts look the way they do on Learn, so a reader who knows one knows the other.

  • Code is the only text set in a monospace face.
  • Hot pink stays on the Download button in the bar.
  • A draft is marked at the top of its page and is not published.
